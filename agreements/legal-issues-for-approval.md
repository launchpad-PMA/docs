# Legal issues requiring approval

**Status:** for counsel. This is not a legal opinion, a tax opinion, or a securities opinion. It does not clear any sentence for publication.

**Drafts reviewed:** the two version 1.0-draft covenants, the unchanged source `membership-covenant`, and the unchanged `SEC-disclaimer`.

**Instruction followed:** disputed provisions were preserved in the drafts and in the redline. They were not rewritten into a "clean" clause. Do not publish either draft, and do not post it on adbongo.io or templeofroots.com, until the items below are decided.

The signing application can collect review copies. While `AGREEMENT_PUBLICATION` is `draft` (the default), every record is stamped as a draft and the pages say the covenant is not in effect.

## 1. Private and exclusive jurisdiction

**Preserved language**

- "a private network state with original and exclusive jurisdiction"
- "exclusive jurisdiction under private membership law, separate from statutory or maritime jurisdictions"
- the waiver of "the right to litigate in public courts"

**Why it needs a decision**

A private contract can choose a governing arrangement for matters the law lets people contract about. It does not create a jurisdiction that pushes aside criminal law, tax law, consumer-protection statutes that cannot be waived, or a court's authority to compel or review arbitration. "Separate from statutory or maritime jurisdictions" will not, by itself, do that work. Calling the Association a "private network state" does not establish a state.

The new Section 4 text, which should stay, says membership does not alter citizenship or other legal status and does not displace mandatory law. That sentence and the preserved exclusive-jurisdiction sentences point in different directions. A signer should not be asked to accept both until counsel picks the operative rule.

**Decision needed**

Keep a private-association and arbitration structure that mandatory law allows, or keep the preserved sentences and accept that they may be unenforceable and may mislead a signer. Do not publish them side by side without an express reconciliation.

## 2. Non-disclosure to public authorities

**Preserved language:** members agree not to disclose Association matters or other members' information "to non-members or public authorities."

**Why it needs a decision**

A confidentiality promise among members is ordinary. A promise not to speak to public authorities is different. It can be unenforceable when a statute, a court order, or a mandatory report requires disclosure. In some settings, asking someone to withhold information from authorities is itself unlawful. The drafts add a records clause that permits disclosure when a written legal duty requires it, and they leave the original bullet in place so the conflict is visible.

**Decision needed**

Narrow confidentiality to non-public information, with a clear exception for mandatory law, safety, and compulsory process. Delete or rewrite the "public authorities" wording only after that decision. It was not deleted in these drafts.

## 3. SEC-related exemption claims

**Preserved language, in the covenant and in `SEC-disclaimer`**

- The Association "operates as a charitable organization under applicable exemptions in the U.S. SEC Code."
- Religious and nonprofit organizations may "conduct activities without classification as securities issuance."
- `SEC-disclaimer` invokes Rule 506(b), Rule 701, and IRC § 508(c)(1)(A), says membership interests "do not constitute securities," and says membership fees or donations are tax-deductible.

**Why it needs a decision**

There is no statute called the "U.S. SEC Code." The Securities and Exchange Commission administers the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, among other laws. Rule 506(b) of Regulation D and Rule 701 are exemptions from *registration*. Using an exemption assumes there may be a security. It is not a finding that the interest is not a security.

Whether a membership, token, or NFT is a security depends on the transaction, including what purchasers are led to expect. Labels such as "governance," "donation," or "membership" do not control that analysis. The source price list and the Genesis Sigil marketing on the current Temple website are facts counsel should look at before anyone says these interests are outside the securities laws.

Tax deduction is not an SEC question. Deductibility depends on the organization's status and on whether the payment is a contribution or a purchase of goods, services, or lodging. A stay, a class, or an LLC service fee is often a purchase, not a gift.

The drafts preserve the covenant sentences and do not adopt `SEC-disclaimer` as an offering document. They also state that an NFT, Patreon payment, or donation does not, under the draft, convey equity or a financial return. That contractual limit is useful and is not a securities opinion.

**Decision needed**

Have securities counsel decide what, if anything, may be said in public. Remove or correct "U.S. SEC Code" before publication. Do not say that membership fees are deductible unless a tax advisor confirms the status and the character of the payment.

## 4. IRC § 508(c)(1)(A) and the auxiliary relationship

**Preserved organizational statement:** Advocacy Launchpad PMA is an unincorporated auxiliary of Temple of Roots, described as a 508(c)(1)(A) nature-based ministry. Donations from glampers or spirit-led services are to be directed to that ministry.

**Why it needs a decision**

Section 508(c)(1)(A) of the Internal Revenue Code excepts churches from the *notice* requirement in section 508(a). It does not, by itself, decide that a particular group is a church, that it is described in section 501(c)(3), or that payments to it are deductible. Church status is a facts-and-circumstances question.

An unincorporated auxiliary does not automatically make every related receipt a church contribution. AdBongo LLC is a separate company. Its consulting fees are not Temple donations merely because a person is also a member.

The drafts keep the source sentence. They add that AdBongo LLC's ownership and management are not stated or changed here. They do not invent owners, managers, or an IRS determination.

**Decision needed**

Tax counsel should confirm what the ministry may say about section 508(c)(1)(A), which receipts are donations, and which receipts belong to AdBongo LLC. Publish the organizational chart counsel approves. Do not let the membership page imply that the LLC is the church or that the church owns the LLC.

## 5. International members and lex mercatoria

**Preserved language:** international members "shall be classified as 'Merchants'" for enforcement under lex mercatoria.

**Why it needs a decision**

Calling a person a merchant does not make them one. The CISG and merchant usages apply, when they apply, because of the transaction and the parties' trade, not because a form says "merchant." A person joining a spiritual or educational association from outside the United States may be a consumer. A clause that reclassifies consumers as merchants can be unenforceable and, in some countries, prohibited.

**Decision needed**

Decide the governing law and the member's capacity with conflicts counsel. Keep the sentence only if counsel wants it preserved as a non-operative recital, or replace it in a later version. It is not removed in these drafts.

## 6. Public-person to private-member status

**Preserved language:** "I have freely chosen to change my legal status from a public person to a private member in the Association."

**Why it needs a decision**

Signing a membership contract creates a contract. It does not change citizenship, tax residence, legal capacity, or a general "public person" status. The new Section 4 text says the opposite in plain words: membership does not, by itself, alter citizenship or other legal status. Leaving both sentences in force would invite a signer, and a later reader, to rely on the wrong one.

**Decision needed**

Choose Section 4 as the operative statement, or explain what "legal status" means in a way that cannot be read as an exit from mandatory law. The status sentence is preserved and flagged. It is not deleted.

## 7. AI-agent governance and consent

**Preserved language:** members agree to AI agents for communication, negotiation, document generation, and enforcement, including autonomous agent-to-agent interaction, inside a framework aimed at "network-state formation."

**Why it needs a decision**

A member can consent to automated messages and to help drafting documents. "Enforcement" and autonomous negotiation are broader. An agent should not be able to spend money, sign a further contract, waive a right, or discipline a member unless a later, specific authorization says so. An agent is not an attorney, a physician, or a spiritual director. Agent output used with members is also a privacy event and should be covered by the records clause.

**Decision needed**

Adopt a short AI schedule: what agents may do, what they may not do, how a person reviews an action, and how to opt out of automated contact. Until that schedule exists, do not treat Section 12 of the drafts as a working delegation. The source wording is preserved underneath that warning.

## 8. Indemnification

**Preserved, and still the clause:** each member indemnifies the Association, officers, affiliates, and other members against any claims arising from participation, including regulatory inquiries, business dealings, and intellectual property.

**Why it needs a decision**

Read literally, a member could owe the Association for a claim that the Association itself caused, and could owe other members as a group. Some liabilities cannot be shifted by contract. A one-sided indemnity of this breadth is a common target in a dispute about unconscionability.

**Decision needed**

The optional alternative in `optional-indemnification-alternative.md` limits the indemnity to third-party claims arising from the member's own conduct, addresses defense expenses, regulatory penalties tied to the member's own acts, intellectual-property claims from material the member supplies, and excludes fraud, willful misconduct, gross negligence, and liabilities that cannot be waived. It is not adopted. Approve it, edit it, or keep the source clause knowingly.

## 9. Arbitration forums

**Preserved options:** Prospera ZEDE (Honduras) and Kleros, elected by the Board of Stewards, plus a waiver of public courts.

**Verification status, November 2026**

- **Kleros** is an operating decentralized arbitration protocol. Whether a Kleros proceeding is "arbitration" under the Federal Arbitration Act, and whether a U.S. court will enforce the result, depends on the clause, the parties, and the procedure. It should not be assumed.
- **Próspera / ZEDE.** Honduras repealed the ZEDE legal framework in 2022. Próspera has been in a public dispute with Honduras, including international claims. Counsel needs a current check on whether a Prospera forum can be seated and whether an award can be enforced, before the clause relies on it.
- The public-court waiver is preserved and is broader than the usual agreement to arbitrate, which still leaves courts available to compel arbitration and for the narrow statutory review of an award.

**What the drafts already do**

They add the sentence the brief required: the parties may mutually agree in writing to an independent arbitrator or another forum. They also include a **proposed, not adopted** fallback: one arbitrator seated in Texas, with appointment under Chapter 171 of the Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code if the parties cannot agree within 30 days. Adopting that fallback would change the preserved court waiver. It is printed so the Board can approve or reject it. It is not silent.

**Decision needed**

Verify Kleros and Próspera in writing. Choose the operative forum. Decide whether to adopt the Texas fallback. Do not leave a waived courthouse as the only door if the named forums cannot hear the case.

## 10. The 100-year term

**Changed, as instructed.** The minimum term of 100 years is removed. Membership continues until resignation or termination. Confidentiality, indemnification, the one-year non-circumvention covenant, and dispute resolution survive according to their terms.

This is an authorized revision, shown in the redline. A 100-year personal covenant would have raised a serious enforceability problem. Counsel should confirm that the surviving obligations are the ones the Association actually wants to survive.

Several of them are health or ceremonial practices with their own consent, safety, and licensing questions. Replacing it would publish an unapproved covenant. After approval, the free foundational covenant has to be the door, and Patreon and NFTs have to be labeled optional.

## 12. Non-circumvention wording

**Preserved on purpose.** The first bullet says members "shall not: Get written permission before soliciting..." That reads as the opposite of a permission requirement. The other bullets are a one-year non-solicit aimed at members, clients, and affiliates.

**Decision needed**

Rewrite the first bullet so it matches the intent. Check the rest for overbreadth. It was not silently corrected.

## 13. AdBongo LLC and the attorney-client line

The drafts state that AdBongo LLC is separate and that this covenant does not describe or change its ownership or management. They do not name owners or managers.

They also state that membership does not create an attorney-client relationship and does not oblige AdBongo LLC to do any work. A Scope of Work template is a separate, unadopted form.

**Decision needed**

Counsel for the LLC should confirm that the covenant and the website do not describe the LLC as the law firm or as the church, and that anyone who needs a professional relationship receives an engagement that says so.

## 14. Electronic signatures

The website method is a typed electronic signature plus a required acknowledgment, after the full covenant is shown. That pattern is built to meet the intent, consent, and retainable-record elements of the federal E-SIGN Act and the Texas Uniform Electronic Transactions Act. It is not a wallet transaction.

**Decision needed**

Counsel should confirm the acknowledgment and the retention period (life of membership plus seven years) against the Association's actual policy. Wallet signatures stay optional.

## 16. Ricardian identification

The drafts now describe a Ricardian form. A lawyer reads the prose. A program reads that same prose, divided at the same headings. The prose has a SHA-256 identifier, and the signature record binds the member's parameters to that identifier. The check recomputes the hashes. It does not move the disputed sentences out of the document, and it does not make them enforceable. A locked draft is still a draft. Publishing the identifier later would publish the hash of the text, not the member's name, email, or signature. Counsel should treat the identifier as a way to know which text was signed, and should still decide the items above before any version is marked approved.
